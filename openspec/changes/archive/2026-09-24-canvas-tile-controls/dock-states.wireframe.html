The dock's three states

One drawing of the ruled model, not a menu. The dock is hidden, bottom-docked or full-canvas, and it is the same tiles with the same rails and the same pins in all three. One invariant moves it without being asked: the spotlight is never shorter than a mini — when the viewport cannot give it that, the dock hides and the spotlight takes the room; when the viewport grows back, the minis return on their own.

Drag the viewport-height slider. That is the whole mechanism: watch the dock hide itself on the way down and come back on the way up, and watch the read-out name the state and the reason. The event log records every interaction and whether it dismissed the dock, so the dismissal rules are testable rather than described.

Every dashed control is a placeholder. The tab, and the MOUNT / CLOSE / HIDE cluster, are drawn as plainly as possible on purpose — which control chrome these become is the next round, and drawing them well here would settle it by stealth. Judge the states and the transitions, not the buttons.

What the drawing asserts

The spotlight's floor is 220px and a mini is 148px — two separate numbers, deliberately. At the shipped app's 1440×900 the canvas field is 550px, so the spotlight has 397px, far clear of its floor. The dock auto-hides when the field drops under 220 + 148 + 8 = 376px, which at this layout is a browser window about 700px tall; above that the minis are not hideable by anyone.

What the 220 buys: a floating dock covers 148px from the bottom, so at the floor the spotlight still shows 72px — its title and a sliver of plot — and stays identifiable and clickable while the dock is up. That holds down to a field of 176px, a window near 500px tall. Below that the title is covered too, and the cluster's Hide is the way out.
