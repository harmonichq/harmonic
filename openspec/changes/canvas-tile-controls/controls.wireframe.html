Evidence-tile controls — five idioms

Three controls live on every tile: alignment (a two-position state — by clock, or by event), pin (a toggle the reader owns) and full (an action). The shipped row spells all three in words. Each option below answers the same question differently: what tells you the alignment at a glance, and what carries pin and full without a label. Hover a tile — the actions are meant to be read at rest and pressed on approach. Both themes are live.

A

Travelling switch

Alignment becomes one physical two-position switch: a recessed track with a raised thumb that slides, the live face inked and the dead face quiet. One target, one movement, and the state is legible from the thumb's position alone — no reading required. Pin and Full are glyphs that rest at 45% and come up when the pointer enters the tile. Cost: the switch is 48px of header on every tile, and its two faces must be glyphs small enough to sit in 24px.

Click the switch. Click a glyph to see its held state.

B

Lamp pair

Two cells in one recessed bezel, each with a glyph and an indicator lamp beneath it; the live one is lit. This is the instrument answer — the control looks like the panel it sits on, and the lit bar is visible in peripheral vision at tile size. Cost: two targets rather than one, and the lit lamp spends accent on a machine state, not on something the reader chose.

C

One glyph, two detents

The alignment control is a single button whose glyph is the current alignment, with two detent dots under it showing which of the two positions it is in. The smallest possible footprint — 24px for all of it — and the header stops being a control strip at all. Cost: a reader who has not learned the glyph pair cannot tell what the other position is without pressing; it leans hardest on the tooltip.

D

Corner overlay

The header is a nameplate and nothing else. All three controls float over the chart's top-right corner on a faint plate, at 28% until the pointer enters the tile. The full header width goes back to the title and caption, and a tile at rest is pure evidence. Cost: the controls sit on the plot, so they cover data in that corner; and a control you cannot see until you hover is a control a keyboard-first or touch reader meets late.

E

Edge rail

The controls leave the header entirely for a 24px recessed rail down the tile's right edge — alignment on top, a hairline, then pin and full, the way switches sit on the side of a rack unit. The header is free, nothing covers the plot, and every tile's controls are in the same place whatever its width. Cost: 24px off every tile's plot width, and the rail repeats four times in a quad arrangement, which is a lot of furniture.
