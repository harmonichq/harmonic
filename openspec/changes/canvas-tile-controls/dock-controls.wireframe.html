The dock's controls

The state machine is ruled and identical in all three: hidden · bottom-docked · full-canvas, the 220 spotlight floor, the raise floor below which the tab mounts instead of docking, and the dismissal rules. Nothing there reopens. The only question is what the reader presses, and where it lives.

Each option is drawn three times — tall in both grounds, and short in light. The short viewport is where these controls work hardest: the dock is hidden by default, the tab is the only way in, and it has to say whether it will dock or mount. Press everything.

C1

The grip, at the dock's right end

kind: the control belongs to the thing it moves

A knurled column at the right end of the dock — the end the row runs off — split into a grow half and a shrink half. It rides the dock in both docked and mounted states, so the control is always attached to the panel it controls and never has to be found somewhere else. Hidden, the tab on the canvas's bottom edge is the way in.

Cost: two pieces of furniture for one state machine — a grip when the dock is up, a tab when it is down — so the reader learns two controls. The grip also eats 14px off the row's right end, which is exactly where the sliced tile is doing its own job of saying "there is more".

Press the grip's halves; press the tab in the short frame.

C2

One handle, on the dock's top edge

kind: one piece of furniture, in every state

A single small handle centred on whatever edge the dock currently presents: the canvas's bottom edge when hidden, the dock's top edge when docked, the top of the grid when mounted. Up grows, down shrinks, and the halves grey out at the ends of the range. One control, learned once, and its position always says where the dock currently is.

Cost: it moves. A reader who learns it at the canvas's bottom edge finds it somewhere else next time, and the thing it is attached to is a boundary rather than an object. Two arrows in 13px is also the smallest hit target of the three.

Press up twice, then down twice, and watch where the handle goes.

C3

A three-position instrument in the rail

kind: the state is named, not gestured

CHARTS: OFF · DOCK · FULL as a segmented control in the instrument rail, beside Window. The state is named and always visible, every transition is one press with no intermediate stop, and it is the same idiom the rail already uses for the thing that re-scopes the surface.

Cost: it reads as a mode switch, which is the exact thing ADR 215 just retired — and it is a permanent instrument for a state the app is supposed to manage on the reader's behalf. On a tall viewport OFF must be disabled, so the control shows a position it will not honour.

Press each position in both the tall and short frames.
