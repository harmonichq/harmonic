The bottom lip — the drawer and the mode, decided together

Grounded on 8877 before any of this was drawn: the chart drawer already exists, already lists every chart ("8 live · 4 seated" — the four seated plus the four that are not), and already opens in both modes; entering Explore force-closes it and it reopens fine. The missing thing is not the capability. It is the way in: a 53px text button at the top right of the canvas head, opening a drawer that emerges from the bottom edge. So none of the concepts below adds a capability — they differ in how much the bottom lip is asked to mean.

Every specimen carries the same lip: a recessed strip along the canvas's bottom edge with a knurled pull, which is where the drawer actually comes from. Press the pull in each. The rail above is as ruled in rounds one and two and does not vary.

D1

One act

kind: the pull is the mode

Pulling the drawer up is entering Explore, and dropping it is returning to Findings. There is no mode word anywhere on the surface: you open the charts, and opening the charts is what "advice off, let me look" has always meant. The lip prints the live count and nothing else. This is the only concept that ends with fewer controls than today.

Needs your sanction — it removes shipped behaviour. Today you can open the drawer while advice is on and drill a chart from it with Findings still live; I exercised that on 8877 this session. Under D1 that state cannot exist. Rule 8 wants it named, dated, quoted in the code, and pinned by a test that fails loudly if it returns.

Other cost: the mode becomes exactly as easy to flip by accident as a drawer pull, on a surface that stages insulin changes.

Pull the lip.

D2

One place, two acts

kind: the lip is an address

The lip is one piece of furniture carrying both jobs at the edge the drawer comes from: the knurled pull opens the charts, and the mode is thrown and read on the same strip, engraved at its right end. Nothing is removed, nothing is coupled — the drawer still opens under advice, and the mode still has an explicit, permanently visible "advice on/off" declaration, which is the thing a dosing-advisory surface should be slowest to give up.

Cost: the mode is still a control, just at a better address — which is the answer you already said was not doing it for you. It also puts two different gestures within 40px of each other on a 22px strip.

Pull the lip; throw the mode at its right end. They are independent.

D3

The roster claims the field

kind: Explore is a place, not a strip

Pulling the lip does not open a 96px strip under the tiles — it hands the whole evidence field over to the chart roster. Explore stops being "advice off" and becomes somewhere you are: the canvas is the roster, at a size where the thumbnails are actually readable, and the tile field returns when you drop the lip. It makes "view all available charts" literal rather than a filmstrip glimpse of it.

Same sanction as D1, plus one. It removes browsing charts with advice on, and it also means the pinned tile field is not on screen in Explore — so the pin schematic in the rail is mirroring a field you cannot see.

Pull the lip and watch the field give way.
