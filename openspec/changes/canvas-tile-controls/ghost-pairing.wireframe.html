Clearing T3's hidden cost

Fill-is-state stays exactly as it is. What is added is the one thing fill could not say on its own: that the clock and the flag are two positions of one control. All three variants share two fixes — the two cells now touch while maximize and pin sit a clear step above them, and the fill transfers on press, the outgoing face draining as the incoming one solidifies. Proximity works at rest; the transfer teaches on the first press. What differs below is only how explicit the grouping is when nothing is moving.

Specimens are the rail at 2.5×, at rest and held (pinned, aligned by event). Tiles are live — hover, then click a cell and watch the fill move.

G1

Proximity and transfer only

Nothing drawn at all: the two cells touch, the actions sit a step above them with the rail's own divider between, and the fill moves from one cell to the other on press. Two things that read as one thing are one thing — grouping by distance is the oldest rule there is, and it costs no ink. Cost: it is inference, not statement; a reader who never presses either cell is relying on the spacing alone to tell them.

G2

A hairline between the cells

The same, plus a single hairline where the two cells meet — a divider inside a control, which is what a two-position control looks like everywhere else in this app. One line, no box, no groove, and it survives at rest when the rail's own material does not. Cost: a divider between two cells is also just a divider — it separates as readily as it joins, and it is one more mark in a margin whose whole point was having none.

G3

A bracket down the inner edge

The same, plus a bracket on the rail's inner edge spanning exactly the two cells — a hairline with a small return at each end, the way a drawing marks an extent. It states the grouping rather than implying it, and it names which cells belong to the control without dividing them from each other. Cost: the most drawing of the three, and a bracket sitting on the rail's own edge hairline needs that edge to not be there at rest — which, in the ghost rail, it isn't.
