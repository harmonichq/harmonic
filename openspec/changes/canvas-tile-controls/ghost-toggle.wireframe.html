Ghost rail — saying which controls hold a state

The rail is settled. What is not: the rail hands a reader four glyphs and only one of them — maximize — is a plain action. Pin holds. Clock and flag are two positions of one thing. Each treatment below says that differently, and the test is whether you can tell without pressing anything.

The three specimens per treatment are the rail alone at 2.5×: at rest (no hover, nothing held), open (pointer on the tile) and held (pinned, and aligned by event). The two tiles under them are live — hover and click.

T1

Grouped in a groove

The alignment pair keeps a recessed track of its own even at rest, when the rest of the rail has no material at all — so the one piece of furniture that survives is the thing that needs explaining, and two cells in one groove read as one control with two positions. The live cell is the panel raised back out of the groove; a held pin takes the same groove around itself. Cost: the rest state is no longer perfectly clean — there is always a small block in the margin.

T2

One marker that travels

Every control that holds a state owns a 2px marker on the rail's inner edge. Alignment's marker is one marker for two cells and it slides between them, so the movement itself says the two are positions of one thing; pin's marker simply lights. Maximize has no marker, ever — a marker means state. Cost: the marker is small, and at rest the pair looks like two ordinary glyphs until you have seen it move once.

T3

Fill is state, stroke is action

One rule for the whole rail: a hollow glyph does something, a solid glyph is something. Pinned fills the pushpin; the live alignment fills its glyph and the dead one stays hollow. Maximize can never fill, so a filled glyph is always a state and never a button you pressed by accident. Cost: it is the one treatment with no shared vocabulary between the two toggles — pin and alignment each just look different when on, so nothing says the clock and the flag are two positions of the same control.

T4

The cell seats

Holding a toggle presses it into the rail — recessed ground, a shadow under its top edge, ink up — the way a latching key stays down. Maximize never seats, so a seated cell is always a held state, and the two alignment cells make the point together because exactly one of them is ever down. Cost: it needs the rail's material to be visible to read as pressed-in, so at rest, with the band ghosted, a seated cell has to carry its own edge.
