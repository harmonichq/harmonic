The handle's form

The mechanism is locked and none of it reopens. This is the handle's own body: silhouette, texture, label, glyphs. I picked all of it unilaterally in the last drawing — the knurl in particular — and it deserves choosing rather than inheriting.

Everything here is drawn at 3×. The handle is 19px tall in the app; at that size a texture is four pixels and cannot be judged on a page. Every dimension is simply tripled, so the proportions are true. Each specimen sits on a scrap of its real ground: trench above, and the pale panel floor or the dock's own top edge below.

A

Knurled tab, word and glyphs

kind: it is a handle and it looks like one

A knurled grip, the word CHARTS, then the glyphs. The texture says "grip me" before any word is read, and the word removes all doubt about what is behind it.

Cost: it is the loudest object on a quiet canvas, and knurling is a texture this app uses nowhere else — it arrives as a new material rather than as the engraved-and-recessed idiom already in use.

B

Engraved plate, no texture

kind: the idiom already in the app, at handle size

No knurl at all. An engraved CHARTS · 11 with the light lower edge the tile nameplates use, on a recessed plate, glyphs at the right. It is the same material as everything else on the surface — nothing new is introduced, and the count earns its place by saying how much is behind it.

Cost: it reads as a label first and a control second, so there is nothing that says "press me" except the hover state — which is exactly the affordance a touch reader never sees.

C

Glyphs only

kind: minimum ink

No word, no texture — one or two glyphs on a small plate. The least ink of the four on a surface whose whole direction has been removing chrome, and the smallest thing that can still be pressed.

Cost: in hidden, a bare glyph on the canvas floor has to carry the entire idea that a dock of charts exists at all — with no word and no texture, a first-time reader has nothing to go on.

D

The depth control

kind: the handle is the readout

Three cells — hidden, docked, mounted — with the live one filled solid. Pressing a cell goes straight there. Control and state in one object, in the fill-is-state language the tile rails already speak, and every transition is one press with no intermediate stop.

Cost: it shows a cell for hidden on a tall viewport, where hiding is not permitted — so it must either grey a cell out or drop to two, and both are the segmented-control problem that sank the rail instrument. It is also the least obviously graspable of the four.

·

The glyphs themselves

kind: four vocabularies for the same four acts

The same four acts — bring up · mount · shrink · put away — drawn four ways. The mechanism needs all four to be distinguishable at 13px, and mount and shrink are the pair most at risk of reading as each other.
