The knurled handle

Ruled and not reopening: knurled tab · knurl centred · no word · glyph only · idiomatic glyphs · the arrow-through-a-line is out. What is left is where the glyphs sit around a centred knurl, and which vocabulary the glyphs come from.

The app already owns two of these glyphs. The shipped evidence-tile rail draws full as four corner brackets facing out and dismiss as four facing in — the same two acts, in the same surface, today. Reusing them for mount and shrink is not choosing a new vocabulary, it is declining to invent a second one. That is the first set below, and it is why the others have to earn their place.

K1

Glyphs flank the knurl

kind: the grip is the middle, the acts are the wings

One glyph each side of the knurl, so the knurl sits at the tab's true centre by construction and the tab is symmetrical about it. Hidden takes the single glyph and the tab shrinks around it, knurl still centred.

Cost: with two glyphs the reader must learn which side does what, and left/right carries no meaning here — neither mount nor hide is naturally "the left one".

K2

Knurl over, glyphs under

kind: grip on top, controls below — two registers

The knurl runs across the top of the tab and the glyphs sit in a row beneath it. Grip and control never compete for the same row, the knurl is centred trivially, and the tab reads as a small plate with a textured lip.

Cost: it is 28px tall in the app against 19px for the others — the tallest object of the set, on the seam where the dock meets the spotlight, which is the one place height is expensive.

K3

Wide tab, glyphs at the ends

kind: a drawer pull with its controls at the corners

A fixed-width tab with the knurl dead centre and the glyphs pushed out to the ends. The tab's width never changes with the number of glyphs, so the handle is the same object in every state and only its contents differ.

Cost: in hidden it carries one glyph and a lot of empty plate — a fixed width means the quietest state pays the widest footprint.

K4

The knurl is the tab

kind: texture is the body, the glyph sits on it

No plate to speak of: the knurl is the whole tab, and the glyph rides its centre on a small clear field. The least added ink of the four — the control is a texture with one mark on it.

Cost: the glyph has to punch a hole in the texture to stay legible, so at 19px it is a mark inside a stripe pattern — the highest risk of the four of reading as noise rather than as a control.

The glyph vocabulary

kind: four acts, four ways — shown at 3× and at true 13px

Four acts: bring up · mount · shrink · put away. Each set is drawn at 3× and again at the 13px it actually ships at, because that second row is where a vocabulary usually fails.
