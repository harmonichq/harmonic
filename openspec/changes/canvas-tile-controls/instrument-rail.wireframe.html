The instrument rail — who owns it, and how far does it run

Measured on 8877 at 1280: the WINDOW segment ends at x=402, the SHOW switch starts at x=991, and the pin schematic sits at x=1158. That is 589px of bare ground through the middle of the row, and the schematic — a live miniature of the tile field, which occupies x 0-850 — is drawn entirely over the Findings column, 308px clear of the field it mirrors. The three instruments are one row and not one rail.

All three variants carry the same instruments and the same 42px height. What differs is the extent of the rail's material and the packing inside it. Specimens are true-size at 1280 and 1024; the inspector column is 430px in both, so the canvas column is 850px and 594px. Controls are live - press them.

R-A

Canvas-width rail

The band runs from the left edge to the canvas/inspector seam and stops; all three instruments pack left inside it, hairline-divided. The rail is then materially the canvas's own chrome, which is what all three instruments actually scope, and the schematic shares an edge with the field it mirrors. Above the inspector the ground stays bare. Cost, measured: the three instruments need 762px and the canvas column at 1024 is 594px, so this variant cannot be built without a narrowing rule - drawn here as shedding the WINDOW and SHOW caption words and the word "pinned" below a 700px column. And the band ends mid-row: the row keeps its 42px height across the whole width, so both panes still start on one line, but 430px of bare ground sits beside the rail at the same height, and that shelf-end has to read as deliberate rather than unfinished.

R-B

Full-width rail, packed left

The band runs the whole window width, one straight shelf under the topbar, but the instruments still pack left inside the canvas column. The top edge stays one line and the rail reads as a single machined piece. Cost, measured: the right 430px is rail material with nothing on it, which claims the rail governs the inspector too - it does not - and an empty third of a shelf invites something to be put there later. At 1024 the instruments run to x=762 against a 594px canvas column, so the schematic is back over the inspector at exactly the width where the tile field it mirrors is smallest.

R-C

Full-width rail, held at both ends

Today's spread, with the material made real: WINDOW at the left edge, SHOW and the schematic at the right edge, band and dividers drawn. Cost: the 589px gap survives, so the rail reads as two clusters that happen to share a height - 519px of it at 1280, still 263px at 1024, so the gap never closes at any supported width; and the schematic stays over the Findings column, which means the miniature and the tile field it mirrors never share an edge - the exact adjacency that makes a schematic legible as a mirror.
