The mode mechanism — four concepts that differ in kind

Ruled and fixed below: the rail is one full-width band; WINDOW is hard left and the pin schematic hard right, both framed to the canvas column. New in this round: the question is no longer where a mode button sits. It is what the mechanism is. Three fresh models were asked for concepts independently; these four survived grounding, and they differ in kind - the mode as a dimension, as no control at all, as occlusion, as power.

Every specimen is live and the point is motion - press the mechanism in each. Two concepts the models liked did not survive contact with settled decisions and are not drawn: making burnt orange the "advice on" bus (orange is reserved for interaction; an advice bus either breaks that reservation or invents the second accent the palette exists to refuse), and raising the chart drawer past a detent to mean Explore (it taxes a cheap peek gesture with an accidental mode flip, in a surface that stages insulin changes).

Scope, stated plainly: all four are larger than slice 3, which is the instrument rail and nothing else. Three of them change behaviour, not clothing. Picking one means either widening this slice or booking it as its own.

C1

The seam is the mode

kind: a dimension

The pane seam becomes a carriage in a channel with two detents and no free travel. Drive it right and the inspector lands as a narrow reading strip; advice does not fit there, so the rank chips, tallies and staging are absent structurally rather than by a rendering rule - the room for them is gone. The canvas gains that width at exactly the moment you switched to reading charts, so the gesture pays you every time.

Collides with a settled decision. .panes already carries var(--side, 430px) with a transition, and the comment under it records the ruling: "one fixed inspector width in every state - a per-state width made the canvas reflow horizontally on each state change". What was rejected was width changing at the reader; this changes it by the reader. That distinction is yours to accept or refuse, not mine.

Other costs, re-priced after grounding: the second layout is cheaper than it first looks - Explore's inspector is a heading and one sentence, which fits a strip comfortably - so what actually has to survive the narrow seat is the crumb and the drilled chart-evidence view, not a queue. The seam does permanently lose its neutrality: it can never later become a genuine user-resizable divider.

Drag or click the knurled grip on the seam. Keyboard: focus it, arrow keys.

C2

The dark slide

kind: occlusion, with an engraved back

A bone plate seated in a channel at the head of the inspector, with a machined pull-tab - a film holder's dark slide. Pushed home, it covers the advisory apparatus and its inner face is engraved with the Explore prompt and the chart roster, so Explore is not a replacement screen but the back of a stowed part. Pull it out and the queue is exposed; the tile field re-seats in the order the slide uncovers.

Cost: a shutter says "hidden", but Explore means "off" - in a surface that stages insulin changes, a mechanism implying the advice is still running behind a cover is the wrong lie to tell. It is also a surface object in a panel built from cuts, which fights the trench idiom, and a pull is worse than a tap on touch.

Pull the tab down, push it back up.

C3

The last thing you touched

kind: no control at all

No mechanism anywhere. The two panes are the switch. Touch a ranked finding and the machine is offering an opinion; touch any chart - the big canvas, any evidence tile - and the opinion retracts. Every click is a whole answer to "what am I doing", so there is no stale state to be stranded in, and the drawer/fullscreen/stack reset stops being a mode side-effect and becomes what it always was: opening a different object. The words survive as two nouns rather than two positions of a switch.

Cost, corrected after grounding: I first priced this as losing "ranked queue, opinion off" - wrong, that state does not exist today either, since the Explore root shows no queue at all. The real cost is that today Explore is a place you can sit with an explicit "Advice off" readout, and under this concept there is no such place and no such declaration - the state is only ever implied by what you last touched. In a surface that advises insulin doses, losing the explicit statement that advice is off is the thing to weigh. It also has no discoverable surface at all, so a reader cannot be told about it, only trained by it, and it is a behaviour change needing your sanction under the slice's rule 8.

Click a finding row, then click a tile or the big chart. Watch the whole surface answer.

C4

The advice key

kind: power, not visibility

A keyed bay milled into the rail beside the pin schematic; the key turns 90° to a detent. The reading is arming, which is exactly right, because Findings is the only mode that can stage a change into a Plan - the gesture costs slightly more than a tab click, in proportion to a switch that also collapses the drawer and resets the stack. Turning it does not reveal the apparatus, it energizes it: ink floods down the queue, top rank first, in a short stagger.

Cost: it is still a control in the rail, which is the thing you were bored of - the invention is in the consequence, not the grab. A removable-looking key also implies a portability it does not have. Cheapest of the four by a wide margin, and the only one that changes no behaviour.

Turn the key in the rail.

+

Hold to peek

orthogonal — rides on any of the four

Two of the three models converged on this independently, and it is not a mode mechanism - it is the free undo that a destructive switch deserves. Hold a key and the opinion lifts for exactly as long as you hold it; release and it seats back. Nothing about your position, scroll, drawer or stack changes: it is a lift, not a navigation. The tell is that the panel goes unlit - every seated plate loses its top highlight and pooled shadow - which nobody can miss and which reads as obviously temporary.

Cost: a held key is hostile to touch and to switch access, so it can only ever be a second gesture, never the mechanism. As a peek it is cheap and it makes every other concept safer.

Press and hold the button under the specimen (or hold the X key over it).
