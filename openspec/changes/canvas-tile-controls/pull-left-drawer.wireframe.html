The pull-left drawer

Corrected premise: the mini row scrolls sideways because the viewport is too short to stack eleven charts vertically — not to spare anyone a list. And the chart list is not a panel inside the inspector: it is a drawer that pulls left out of the dock and floats over the interface. That lifts the 430px ceiling both earlier rounds were drawn against, so thumbnails can be whatever size the drawer chooses to be.

Still ruled, not reopening: the row holds ranked findings only, a pin keeps a chart left-most, the row is dropped whole when the pane cannot draw one legible mini, and the scroll affordance is a sliced fourth tile. The grip is the same in all three specimens — a vertical knurl on the dock's left edge, because the knurl's direction is the pull's direction. What differs is what comes out of it.

Measured at 1440×900: canvas pane 1010×790, mini 326×145, inspector 430 wide, dock 429×98. Specimens are 0.62×, and each option's own measured size and scroll behaviour is stated in its claim — taken from the rendered specimen, not asserted. A thumbnail here is ~150×42, well under the 280px a mini needs, which is fine: a thumbnail is for recognition, a mini is for reading.

D1

The dock grows a wing

kind: the row, extended — same band, same height

The drawer is the dock's own height and slides left along the bottom of the window, floating over the mini row it extends. Same band, same reading posture, same left-to-right order: it is the row with the lid off, and a reader who has been scrolling the row finds the identical charts in the identical order, all at once.

Cost: measured 1026×100, and it scrolls in both axes at eleven charts — the drawer does not escape the constraint that created it, it reproduces it. It also covers the row, so pinning happens over the thing being reordered.

Press the grip. Scroll inside the drawer.

D2

A floating card

kind: a panel that takes only the space it needs

A card hinged at the dock's top-left corner, sized to its contents and no more. Measured 710×368, four columns. It floats over the canvas but leaves the glucose strip showing, so the surface underneath stays legible as context — the only one of the three that keeps the focal chart in view while you pin against it.

Cost — measured, and it sinks the pitch: this was drawn to fit everything without scrolling. It does not. Nine ranked charts in four columns is three rows, plus the Watching header and its two reads makes five, and it still scrolls at 368 tall. I grew it twice and it still scrolls; the height that would fit is about 470, which is D3 lying on its side. Its size is a decision rather than a consequence, and thirty charts needs a rule this option does not have.

Press the grip. Pin two charts; dismiss and look at the row.

D3

The full-height shelf

kind: a proper drawer — pane height, veiled behind

Full pane height, hinged on the dock's left edge, sliding left to a fixed width with the interface veiled behind it. Vertical space is exactly what the row was short of, so this is the one option that answers the actual constraint. Measured 532×432: eleven charts in three columns, the Watching group included, with no scrolling at all and room left over — and thirty would scroll down a shelf that is built to be scrolled down.

Cost: the veil makes it a place you go rather than a thing you glance at — closest of the three to the second mode ADR 215 just retired, and the only one that hides the focal chart you are pinning against.

Press the grip. Press the veil to dismiss.
