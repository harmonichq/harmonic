Edge rail — five ways to run it

Every variant: maximize at the top right, pin under it, alignment as a lit clock-or-flag pair, and the whole header given back to the title. The tall tile and the quad tile are shown together because they fail differently — the quad tile pays real plot width for the rail, and the tall tile is where an always-drawn rail becomes a long empty band. Click things; both themes are live.

Rail width is 24px throughout. On the 560px tile that is 4% of the plot; on the 252px tile it is 10%.

R1

Full rail

The baseline, corrected: a recessed band the whole height of the tile, maximize at the top, pin under it, alignment at the bottom of the group. Every tile's controls are in the same place at the same size regardless of arrangement, and the band reads as the machined edge of the instrument. Cost: on a tall tile most of the band is empty — look at the focal tile below and decide whether that reads as deliberate or as waste.

R2

Short rail

The same rail, but the recessed material stops where the controls stop and closes with a hairline; below it the gutter is plain panel. No empty band on a tall tile, and the group still reads as one seated block. Cost: the tile now has two different right edges — recessed at the top, flush below — and in a quad arrangement that seam lands at a different height than the plot's own furniture.

R3

Corner cap + rail

Maximize gets its own cap in the header band at the very top right, closed underneath by a hairline; pin and alignment live in a flush rail below it. This separates the one control that takes the tile somewhere else from the two that configure it in place. Cost: three edges on one corner (cap, rail, tile), which is a lot of drawing for three glyphs.

R4

Ghost rail

The gutter is always reserved but never drawn — at rest there are three quiet glyphs in the margin and no band at all, so nothing competes with the plot; the recess, its edge and its divider fade in when the pointer enters the tile or a control takes focus. The pinned state stays lit at rest, because that one is the reader's own. Cost: at rest there is no visible boundary, so the glyphs float; and the whole idiom depends on hover, which a touch reader does not have.

Hover a tile to bring the rail up.

R5

Alignment at the axis

Alignment is not a tile action — it says what the x-axis is — so it leaves the rail and sits at the bottom right of the plot, on the axis it governs, marked the way the canvas marks a live mode. The rail shrinks to two glyphs: maximize, then pin. Cost: two control locations per tile instead of one, and on the quad tile the axis control is close enough to the plot to read as part of the data.
