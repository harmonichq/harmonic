The chart filmstrip is chrome, not a fourth row of charts

The idea. The strip is a navigator — the thing you pick the next chart from — and it has been dressed as four more evidence panels of equal standing, which is why no amount of tinting its well will stop it fighting the spotlight. It joins the app's chrome bar role instead, the one theme.css already gives the top bar and the status bar, and carries its miniature plots as indicators. The recess then costs nothing to state: chrome is a different material from the sheet, so the strip is a band of the app's frame laid into the canvas floor, evergreen against bone in light and the footer's own graphite in dark. The cells give up the nameplate plate, the subtitle and three of their four controls; what is left is a name printed on the chrome and a bright window cut into it.

The cost. The strip stops being a place a chart can be worked on: with mount, full-screen and the alignment cells gone from the cell, promoting to the spotlight is the only thing a click in the strip does, and a reader who wants a mini full-screen pays two gestures instead of one. It also spends the chrome-bar role's meaning — a third band in the frame's material inside the working surface — so if that role is ever restyled, the canvas floor moves with the top bar, and in light the canvas now carries a saturated evergreen band where it previously carried none.
