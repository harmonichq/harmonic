The chart filmstrip, milled rather than tinted

The idea. Depth was the right answer and it was executed as a colour step, so the eye got a darker rectangle instead of a cut. This specimen commits to the recess: the pocket floor steps far enough from the field to be a different surface — away from it downward in light, where the frames already own the brightest ground the theme has, and upward in dark, where the field is already near-black and there is nowhere below to go — and the walls of the cut are drawn over the frames by a pointer-inert plate laid at the band's exact geometry, because an inset shadow on the row paints behind its own children and so never reached the place the eye actually looks. One dark line at the cut with a lit bevel on the rim either side of it, plus a lit chamfer on the pocket's own bottom wall, is what gives the edge a thickness — a single dark line is a drawn rectangle. The floor ends up one step below the frames in both themes, which is the one sentence the whole treatment rests on.

The cost. It is the most shadow machinery on the canvas, and it buys that read with a second element the row's own box cannot supply: an overlay pinned to the band's geometry, which means the 145px band, the 8px gutter and the 2px radius are now stated in two places and must move together. It also lands a soft wall over the sliced fourth frame and over the leftmost nameplate, so the strip's darkest ink sits where its quietest type is.
