WIREFRAME — NO FIDELITY CLAIM — NOT LOCKABLE

The filmstrip stops being a well and becomes a ruled register

The idea. Depth is the wrong lever here — a well that must be legible on bone and on graphite is two different fictions, and in light there is no darker step available that does not also dim the plots. So the strip claims no depth at all: it runs full bleed off three edges of the field, its cells butt together as apertures in one continuously ruled band, its nameplates align into a single horizontal rule crossing the whole strip, and the 34px canvas spine carries a frame index. Subordination comes from what the strip is — substrate, ruled, ordered, indexed — against a spotlight that is still a raised plate floating inside its margins.

The cost. It spends the canvas's stated depth vocabulary: the strip no longer says raised-or-cut in the language the tiles and the field speak, so a reader who learned that vocabulary elsewhere on the surface gets no answer from the strip, and the whole subordination rests on a reading of geometry that a busy plot butted against its neighbour can blur. It also adds one mark the ADR's "no badge" clause did not sanction — the frame index — and buys the ruled grid by giving every cell a fixed 22px nameplate band it can no longer trade away.
