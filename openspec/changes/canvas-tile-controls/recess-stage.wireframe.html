Raise the stage, and the strip stops needing a well

The idea. Depth is a relationship, not a property, and every round so far has spent it on the wrong end: in light the cells already sit on the brightest surface the theme owns, so pushing the strip down had nowhere to go and the well resolved to the field's own #E7E4DC. This specimen spends the whole budget above the dock instead — the spotlight stands in the field with air on three sides, takes the only surface at full brightness, and casts a real shadow onto the strip below it; the strip runs flush to the field's floor and off its right wall like the floor of the room the plate is standing in. The strip's own treatment is then subtraction: its tray ground, ring, inset lip and padding are all deleted, and what remains is the shipped cells on the field's own ground with the field's own gutter between them.

The cost. The cells give up a notch of ground brightness to buy the stage its exclusivity — one step, measured to keep the plot ground readable, but it is the plot's ground and not the chrome's, so a faint trace in a thumbnail is the first thing that suffers; and a composition that rests on a cast shadow rests on something a very short viewport compresses, because when the stage is near its 220px floor there is less plate above the strip for the shadow to fall from.
