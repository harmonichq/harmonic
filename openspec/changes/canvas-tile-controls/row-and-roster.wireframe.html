The row and the roster

The amendment rules the mechanism and leaves two surfaces undrawn. Ruled and not reopening: the row holds ranked findings only, a pin keeps a chart left-most, the row is dropped whole when the pane cannot draw one legible mini, and the scroll affordance is a sliced fourth tile — no fade, no chevron. Everything below is the clothing on that.

Measured, at a 1440 viewport: canvas pane 1010px, mini 326×145, inspector 430px, dock 429×98. The specimens are that at 0.62×, so the roster's real width is 430px and a row tile's real width is 326px — judge the density there, not at what the screenshot shows.

Q1 — the roster is 430px of inspector. What is a row of it?

It lists eleven charts here: nine ranked findings and, under them, the two Watching reads the row will not seat on its own. Pinning is the only way one of those reaches the canvas, so the pin has to be reachable on every row. Press a pull, then press pins. Pinned charts turn up left-most in the row behind the sheet — close it and look.

R1

Named list

kind: a register you read, not a gallery you browse

One line per chart: name, pin. Eleven charts fit without scrolling, so the whole roster is one glance and the Watching group is visible from the top. The name is the identifier — these are findings, and their names are already the words the queue two inches away uses.

Cost: "browse every registered chart" becomes reading a list. A reader who does not recognise a chart by name has to seat it to find out what it draws.

Press the pull; pin "Correction on active insulin"; close and look at the row.

R2

Name over a wide thumbnail

kind: one chart per row, at reading width

Each row is the name and a 400px-wide, 35px-tall slice of the chart under it. At that aspect a glucose trace or a run scatter is actually recognisable — it is the same wide-and-short proportion the mini tiles have — and the roster still reads top to bottom.

Cost: about four rows visible at once, so eleven charts means scrolling a panel opened to avoid scrolling a row. Eleven live ECharts thumbnails also mount on open, where R1 mounts none.

Press the pull and scroll the sheet.

R3

Two-column thumbnails

kind: a contact sheet

Two columns of small thumbnails, six rows. More charts visible at once than R2, and it reads as a gallery — the thing the roster is nominally for.

Cost: at 430px a column is ~205px wide and the thumbnail is ~200×26; that is below the width this very change just declared too narrow for a mini to be legible. A thumbnail nobody can read is R1 with extra ink.

Press the pull; try to tell two charts apart by their pictures.

Q2 — the pull sits on the dock. What is it?

Same roster in all three (R1's list, so only the pull differs). The dock is pane furniture — mounted once, repainted in place, never scrolled away — so whatever the pull is, it inherits that permanence. Press each one.

P1

A knurled lip

kind: a drawer pull, and it looks like one

A recessed full-width strip under the dock's last line, knurled, centred. It is a handle — the one control on the surface that says "there is something behind this" by looking like something you grip.

Cost: it is the loudest thing in the dock, competing with the Trial line, which is the one object the dock exists to report.

Press it.

P2

The dock's next line

kind: not furniture — another thing the dock reports

Trial · Profile · All charts · 11, in the dock's own line grammar, with its count where the other lines put their value. The dock already reports what is being watched; the roster is one more standing fact about this surface.

Cost: the dock's lines are reports and this one is a control. Nothing distinguishes them but the hover, so a reader may not find it at all.

Hover the dock's three lines; only one of them presses.

P3

A tab on the dock's top edge

kind: an index tab on a drawer that is already shut

A small tab riding the dock's upper edge at the right, the way a filed card stands proud of the box. It reads as a control without taking a dock line, and its position says the sheet comes up from here.

Cost: new furniture in a 22px band, and it overlaps the queue's last row — on a short queue it floats over blank panel, which looks like a mistake.

Press the tab.

Q3 — the sliced fourth tile is ruled. Does anything mark HOW FAR the row runs?

The slice says "there is more". It does not say how much more, or where you are in it. Three answers. Scroll each row.

S1

The slice alone

kind: nothing added

The row overflows and the field's edge cuts it. No scrollbar, no rule, no count. The cheapest possible answer, and the one the amendment already ruled sufficient.

Cost: with eleven charts a reader has no idea whether the row is four long or forty, and no sense of position after scrolling. On a trackpad-less machine there is also no visible thing to drag.

Scroll the row (shift-wheel, or drag on a trackpad).

S2

A gauge under the row

kind: extent and position, in one hairline

A 2px hairline under the row whose filled length is the visible fraction and whose position is the scroll position. It answers both questions at once in less ink than a scrollbar, and it is machined furniture rather than a browser control.

Cost: a line under the row on a field that just lost the schematic — it is the schematic's ghost, re-earned or not.

Scroll the row and watch the gauge.

S3

A persistent thin scrollbar

kind: the platform's own answer

A 5px scrollbar, always shown rather than overlay-faded. Draggable, which neither other option is, and every reader already knows what it is.

Cost: it is the one piece of un-machined chrome on the canvas, it renders differently on every platform, and it takes 5px off the 145px the minis have.

Scroll or drag the bar.
