The row expands

Corrected again, and this is the version that matters: the pull does not open a roster, a list, or a contact sheet. It grows the row itself. Same tiles, same rail on each, same pins, same order, same sideways scroll — just bigger, floating over the focal chart. One interface at two sizes, so there is no second representation to learn, keep in sync, or name.

The grip is the same in all three and it sits at the row's right end — the end the row runs off — as a vertical knurl with a chevron that turns to say which way it will go. The Watching reads sit past a divider at the far right of the expanded row, still reachable only by pinning. Everything ruled earlier holds: ranked findings seat themselves, a pin keeps a chart left-most, the sliced tile is the scroll affordance.

Measured in the shipped app at 1440×900: canvas pane 1010×790, the row's band 1010×145, focal tile 994×381, each mini 326×145, and 3.35 minis visible before the slice. The specimens are schematic, not to scale — their inspector is proportionally wider than the real 430px, so read the ratios below, which are measured off the rendered specimens, and not their pixel values as app pixels. Every option's collapsed tile is identical; only the expansion differs.

E1

It grows out of the right side

kind: the row unrolls leftward from its grip

The band detaches at the grip and grows left and up, becoming a tall panel against the field's right edge, over the focal chart. The tiles get taller, the row still runs sideways, and the grip stays exactly where the hand left it.

Cost, measured: it is the only option that makes the row narrower in exchange for height — tiles grow 1.22× wide and 1.39× tall, but only 2.1 are visible where 3.35 were before, so expanding shows you fewer charts at once than the collapsed row did. The row also leaves the place it lives: it starts as a band across the bottom and ends as a block on the right, so the gesture is a move as well as a growth.

Press the grip. Scroll inside. Press again.

E2

It comes up from the bottom

kind: the band rises in place, still one row

The band stays exactly where it is and grows upward over the focal chart. Nothing moves sideways, nothing relocates: the tile under your pointer before the press is the tile under it after. Taller tiles, same single row, same sideways scroll, and the grip has not moved a pixel.

Cost, measured: it spends the growth on tile size and almost none of it on reach — 1.22× wider, 1.10× taller, and 2.7 visible against 3.35 collapsed. It buys legibility and actually costs overview, which may be the opposite of what the pull is for.

Press the grip; note that nothing moves sideways.

E3

It comes up, and wraps

kind: the band rises and the row becomes rows

Same upward growth in the same place, but the tiles wrap instead of running on: three across, as many rows as it takes, scrolling down instead of sideways. The whole set is visible at once — which is the thing the row is short of, because the viewport is too short — and the tiles stay the same width they already were, so nothing has to be re-read at a new size.

Cost: the scroll axis flips under the press. A reader who has scrolled sideways to chart seven presses the grip and their position is meaningless in the new axis, and the sliced tile that said "there is more" is replaced by a different affordance. Measured, it is the only one that pays for its growth: 1.09× wider, 1.25× taller, 3.1 across per row and every remaining chart one short vertical scroll away rather than seven sideways.

Press the grip; scroll down inside; press again.
