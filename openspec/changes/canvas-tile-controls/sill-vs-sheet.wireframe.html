The sill, and the contact sheet

Two concepts for the one open question: what moves the dock between hidden, bottom-docked and full-canvas. The state machine underneath is ruled and identical in both. Three earlier attempts were rejected — a side grip (collides with the sliced tile, and reads bolted-on once mounted), a moving handle (must be re-found), and a segmented mode switch in the rail (reads as the mode this change just retired).

Authored at size. Nothing here is scaled — the last round used a CSS zoom to enlarge the frames and it corrupted flex layout rather than magnifying it, which is what left a pale band under every specimen. These are the real proportions, so what you see is what the geometry does.

S

The Sill

kind: the canvas floor is a machined ledge with a depth gauge

A permanent 26px inset ledge across the canvas pane's floor: CHARTS · 11 engraved at the left, three depth ticks engraved at the right with the live one filled solid, and a knurled pad centred, split into an upper and a lower half. It never moves — in hidden it sits on the canvas floor, in docked the tile row rests on it, in mounted the grid scrolls above it and the sill is the one thing that stays. Your bottom-edge CHARTS tab is not discarded here: it is the sill's centre pad, grown into the pane's architecture instead of stuck onto it, and the filled tick says which of three depths you are at without a word of hue.

Cost: it spends 26px of canvas permanently — including in hidden, where the whole argument was "the spotlight alone" — and it is a new full-width band in a change whose entire direction has been deleting chrome.

Press the pad's halves. Watch the ticks fill.

B

Both — the sill that folds

kind: one ledge, at two widths, according to what the canvas can afford

The sill, but it folds when the canvas cannot afford it. On a tall viewport it is the full ledge: engraved CHARTS · 11, knurled pad, depth ticks. On a short one the caption and the ledge go and only the pad survives, floating on the field's floor — which is exactly the knurled tab you said was right, and costs nothing but its own height. And because mounted always has the whole pane to spend, the full sill is always present once mounted, so there is a visible, labelled way out of full canvas at every viewport size.

Cost: the control changes shape across a breakpoint the reader cannot see, so its size is a fact about the window rather than about the surface — and a reader who learns the full ledge meets a bare pad on a laptop and has to recognise it as the same thing.

Compare the tall and short frames — same control, two widths. Mount from the short frame and watch the full sill appear.

C

The Contact Sheet

kind: mounted is not a state, it is a picker you are inside

Hidden and docked are the surface's two real states, moved by the knurled CHARTS tab on the canvas's bottom edge — the shape you already said was right. Press the tab again from docked and it mounts, and mounted then carries no chrome whatsoever: you came there to choose, so choosing is the exit. Click any chart and it takes the spotlight and the sheet closes, which the ADR already rules. The pane's own edge lights while you are inside it, so the state is legible without a control naming it.

Cost: a reader who mounts to browse, changes their mind, and wants out without changing their focal chart has only unadvertised exits — Escape, or a click on the lit edge. The specimen prints that hint in the corner; whether the real thing can is a question this option has to answer.

Press the tab, then press it again. Click a chart to leave.
